To manage the **`iam.managed.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`** organization policy constraint, you must have the **Organization Policy Administrator** role (`roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin`) at the organization level[1][2][3].

If you are blocked by this policy, here is the standard process to override it for a specific project or the entire organization[1][3]:

### 1. Verify Your Permissions
*   You must be assigned the **Organization Policy Administrator** role at the **organization level**[2][3]. 
*   This role often does not appear in the role list for individual projects; you must select your organization in the resource picker (top-left of the Google Cloud Console) to grant or verify this role[4][2][3].

### 2. Override the Policy
Once you have the required permissions, you can modify the policy at the project or organization level[3]:
1.  Go to the **[Organization Policies](https://console.cloud.google.com/iam-admin/orgpolicies)** page in the Google Cloud Console[5][3].
2.  Use the filter to search for **`iam.managed.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`**[1][3].
3.  Click on the policy name to open its details[1][3].
4.  Click **Manage policy**[1][3].
5.  Select **Override parent's policy** (this is critical if the policy is being inherited from the organization)[6][7][3].
6.  Under **Enforcement**, select **Off** (or "Not enforced")[8][5][3].
7.  Click **Set policy**[8][3].

### Important Troubleshooting Notes
*   **Dual Constraints:** Google Cloud often utilizes both a legacy constraint (`iam.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`) and a managed constraint (`iam.managed.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`)[1][9]. If you disable one but the block persists, ensure you have checked and overridden **both**[1][9].
*   **Propagation Time:** Changes to organization policies are not always instantaneous and may take a few minutes to propagate across your projects[10].
*   **Security Recommendation:** Service account keys are long-lived, portable credentials that pose significant security risks[5][11]. Where possible, consider alternatives like **Workload Identity Federation** or service account impersonation to avoid using static keys entirely[5][11].
