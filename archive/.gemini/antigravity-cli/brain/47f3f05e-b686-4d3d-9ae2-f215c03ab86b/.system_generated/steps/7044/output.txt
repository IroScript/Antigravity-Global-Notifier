No, you cannot grant yourself the **Organization Policy Administrator** role (`roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin`) at the **project level**.

The `roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin` role is specifically designed to be granted at the **Organization** resource level. Because it provides the authority to define restrictions that impact the entire hierarchy (including folders and projects), it is not available for assignment at the project level.

### Key Points:
*   **Hierarchical Restriction:** The lowest-level resource where you can grant the `roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin` role is the **Organization**.
*   **Project Owner Limitations:** Being a Project Owner (`roles/owner`) only grants you full control over that specific project. It does **not** grant you the elevated permissions required to manage organization-level policies or to assign yourself organization-level roles.
*   **How to Get Access:** To obtain this role, you must have an administrator of the Organization (typically someone with Organization Administrator permissions or a Super Admin) grant it to you at the **Organization level**. Once granted, it will inherit down to the projects you manage.
*   **Alternative for Specific Scopes:** If your organization needs to delegate this power more granularly (e.g., to a specific team for only a subset of projects), some organizations use **IAM Conditions** with tags to restrict the scope of the `roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin` role, but this must still be configured by an administrator at the organization level.

If you are currently blocked by an organization policy and do not have the required permissions to change it, you will need to contact your Google Cloud Organization Administrator to request the necessary role or have them adjust the policy for you.
