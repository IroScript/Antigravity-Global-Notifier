To override the `iam.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation` organization policy, you must have the **Organization Policy Administrator** role (`roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin`) assigned at the **organization level**[1][2][3].

### Important Prerequisites
*   **Permissions:** You must have the `roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin` role[1][4]. If you do not see this option, ensure you are selecting your **Organization** in the Google Cloud Console project picker, not a specific project[5][3].
*   **Hierarchy:** If the policy is enforced at the organization level, you can typically override it for a specific project by creating an exception[1][6]. However, if the policy is configured to prevent overrides or is enforced strictly, you may need to consult your organization's administrator[7][8].

### Steps to Override the Policy
1.  **Navigate to Organization Policies:**
    *   Go to the [Organization Policies](https://console.cloud.google.com/iam-admin/orgpolicies) page in the Google Cloud Console[1][9].
    *   In the resource selector at the top-left, **select your Organization** (the top-most node in your hierarchy)[1][3].
2.  **Find the Constraint:**
    *   In the filter bar, search for `iam.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`[10][1].
    *   Click on the policy name to open its details page[1].
3.  **Override the Policy for a Project:**
    *   Click **Edit**[1][2].
    *   Under **Policy source**, select **Override parent's policy**[11][1].
    *   Change the enforcement setting from **Enforced** to **Not enforced** (or customize it to exclude specific projects if supported)[1][6].
    *   Click **Save**[1][12].

### If the Override Option is Grayed Out
If you have the correct roles but still cannot override the policy, it is likely because:
*   **Strict Enforcement:** The organization policy may have been set at a level that explicitly forbids further overrides[7][8].
*   **Missing Org-Level Permissions:** You might be trying to manage the policy at the project level instead of the organization level[5][3]. Ensure you have navigated to the organization's policy settings[1][3].

If you are still unable to make changes, you must contact your organization's Google Cloud Administrator to request a temporary exception or a change to the policy[8].
