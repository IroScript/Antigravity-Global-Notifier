The organization policy `iam.managed.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation` is part of Google Cloud's **"secure-by-default"** baseline, which is automatically enforced for all organizations created on or after May 3, 2024[1][2][3].

This policy is designed to improve security by preventing the creation of long-lived, user-managed service account keys, which are a common vector for security compromises[4][5][6].

### Can you opt out or disable it?
Yes, you can disable or override this policy, but it requires specific administrative privileges[7][8]. Because it is an organization-level policy, you cannot override it if you only have project-level permissions[9][10].

### How to Disable or Modify the Policy
To modify this constraint, you must have the **Organization Policy Administrator** (`roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin`) or **Organization Administrator** (`roles/resourcemanager.organizationAdmin`) role[7][10][8].

1.  **Navigate to Organization Policies:** Go to the Google Cloud Console, select your organization, and navigate to **IAM & Admin > Organization policies**[10][8].
2.  **Locate the Constraint:** Search for `iam.managed.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`[10][8].
3.  **Manage/Edit Policy:** Click on the policy name. If it is enforced at the organization level, you can choose to:
    *   **Disable it:** Change the enforcement state to "Off" (or "Not enforced")[8].
    *   **Create an Exception:** Depending on your needs, you may be able to set the policy to apply only to specific projects or folders rather than the entire organization[6][10].

### Important Considerations
*   **Legacy vs. Managed Constraints:** Google is transitioning from legacy policies (e.g., `iam.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`) to managed versions (`iam.managed.disableServiceAccountKeyCreation`)[11][10]. If you are still blocked after modifying one, **check if both the legacy and managed versions are enforced**[10]. You may need to disable both to fully enable key creation[10].
*   **Security Recommendation:** Google strongly recommends using more secure alternatives to long-lived keys, such as **Workload Identity Federation** or service account impersonation, rather than disabling this policy[7][5][12]. If you must use keys, ensure they are rotated frequently and managed according to security best practices[3][5].
